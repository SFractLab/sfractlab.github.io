Julia sets inside the Mandelbrot set

Embedded Julia sets

Level: IntermediateBuilds on Minibrots & self-similarity

Deep in the filaments of the Mandelbrot set, zooms meet small islands that look like Julia sets. Zoom toward the middle of one and its two-fold pattern turns four-fold, then eight-fold, then sixteen-fold, until a minibrot appears at the center. This chapter follows one such zoom with exact numbers and explains it: around a minibrot, the Mandelbrot set copies the Julia set of the minibrot’s center, and every pass of the orbit near the critical point doubles the copies.

An island in the filaments

People exploring the set with computers found these islands long before they were explained: according to Wolf Jung, as Kawahira and Kisaka report, Robert Munafo and Jonathan Leavitt observed them in computer experiments decades ago. Munafo’s encyclopedia of the Mandelbrot set calls them embedded Julia sets: structures of filaments that look like a Julia set and lie close to a larger minibrot, compared with that minibrot’s size (Mu-Ency, “Embedded Julia Set”). Other names in use are Julia islands and medallions.

The example in the figure lies next to the real minibrot of period 3, whose center is at −1.7548776662 (the minibrots chapter takes it apart). Like the whole set, this copy has a seahorse valley where its cardioid meets its largest bulb, which has period 6 and touches the cardioid at −1.7685292. Hanging into that valley, 0.0016 above the point of contact, is an island about 4·10−6 long. At its center lies a minibrot of period 101 whose center is at

c0 ≈ −1.7686678630 + 0.0016455803i,

only 2.5·10−9 long from its cusp to the tip of its antenna. The figure zooms from the valley to this minibrot, two million times in all.

Two things stand out. The island is a copy of a Julia set, with the minibrot where that Julia set has its critical point 0; the next section shows which Julia set and why. And inside the island, the pattern repeats around the center 2, 4, 8, 16, … times, closer and closer to the minibrot. Munafo calls this doubling of the symmetry bifurcation (Mu-Ency, “Bifurcation”); a later section explains it. It is not period doubling: every ring surrounds the same minibrot of period 101, and the periods of the nodes grow by 101 from ring to ring, 104, 205, 306, 407. Period doubling, in the minibrots chapter, attaches components of periods p, 2p, 4p, ….

A Julia set around the critical point

Take a parameter c near the center c0. The orbit of 0 under z2 + c0 returns exactly to 0 after 101 steps, so the orbit under z2 + c returns close to it:

z101(c) ≈ b′ · (c − c0), b′ = dz101/dc at c0 ≈ −27,078 + 65,614i.

From step 101 on, the orbit simply continues from the point z101(c), so c belongs to M exactly when that point has a bounded orbit under z2 + c: when it lies in the filled Julia set of c. Close to c0, that filled Julia set differs little from the one of c0. Near c0, therefore, the Mandelbrot set looks like the filled Julia set of c0 around 0, shrunk by |b′| ≈ 70,982 and turned by the argument of b′, 112.4°. The figure compares the two, with the Julia picture 70,982 times as wide as the island’s.

This explains the two-fold symmetry. The comparison is with the Julia set around its critical point 0, and around 0 every quadratic Julia set is symmetric under the half turn z ↦ −z, because both points have the same image (Julia sets). At a Misiurewicz point the Mandelbrot set looks instead like the Julia set around the critical value c (Tan Lei’s theorem), where nothing forces such a symmetry.

Why does the Julia set of c0 look like a whole Julia set around 0? Because c0 lies near the period-3 minibrot. For the first 90 steps, the orbit of 0 comes back to within 0.11 of 0 every third step, as the orbit of the period-3 center does. Near 0, three steps of z2 + c then act like one step of another quadratic map z2 + c*, the renormalization that makes the period-3 minibrot a copy of M, and the points whose orbits keep returning close to 0 every three steps form a small copy of a quadratic Julia set. The parameter c* is the point of the whole set that corresponds to the island’s place beside the period-3 minibrot. Read through the minibrot’s size and orientation, the island’s place corresponds to about −0.72 + 0.09i, at the mouth of the whole set’s seahorse valley, which is where the island is in the period-3 copy. Munafo describes it the same way: an embedded Julia set is shaped like the Julia set whose parameter lies, relative to the whole set, where the island lies relative to its nearby minibrot, very close to that Julia set but never exactly equal.

Islands that blend in

Without a larger minibrot nearby, the copy is of a Julia set of the location itself. The three-way branch point of the 1/3 limb, c ≈ −0.1010963638 + 0.9562865108i (Special points), has a minibrot of period 31 at c0 ≈ −0.1006662519 + 0.9563357169i, 4.33·10−4 away and 6.7·10−10 long. Its embedded Julia set copies a Julia set close to that of the branch point, a dendrite, and so it blends into the filaments around it. The match is just as exact: here b′ ≈ −48,008 − 20,058i, and the filled Julia set of c0 0.0624 wide around 0 matches the Mandelbrot set 1.2·10−6 wide around c0, turned by about 23°. Why the copies double

The minibrots chapter derives the return map near 0. For c near the center c0 of a minibrot of period p, here 101, the p-th iterate near 0 is, to good accuracy,

g(z) = b(c) + Az2, b(c) = zp(c), A = 2z1 · 2z2 ⋯ 2zp−1,

with the zk taken on the orbit of c0. In the coordinate w = Az it becomes w ↦ w2 + C, with C = Ab(c) ≈ (c − c0)/s and s = 1/(Ab′). For the island, A ≈ −12,409 − 1,508i and s ≈ (5.53 + 9.82i)·10−10. The minibrot is roughly c0 + s·M: 2.25 |s| ≈ 2.5·10−9 long, as M is 2.25 long from −2 to 1/4, and tilted by the argument of s. Deep-zoom programs use this size estimate to frame a minibrot before they draw it.

A parameter outside the minibrot has a large C. Its orbit comes back near 0 every p steps, at w = C, then C2 + C, then (C2 + C)2 + C, …: each pass squares, and the values grow like C, C2, C4, … until the orbit leaves the neighborhood of 0; where it lands then decides whether it escapes. A number has two square roots, so whatever the orbit does after a pass happens at twice as many parameters as the same behavior one pass earlier. The first pass already squares, which is why the island is two-fold. One more pass gives four copies of each of its features, then eight, sixteen, ….

The nodes show it exactly. The island’s two nodes have period 104: their orbits follow the orbit of c0 for 101 steps, back to within 0.092 of 0, and then reach 0 in three more steps, in the rhythm of the period-3 minibrot. A node that makes one more pass is 0.0026 from 0 after 101 steps and 0.092 after 202, and reaches 0 three steps later: it has period 205. Newton’s method, started at the square roots, finds four such nodes; then 8 of period 306 and 16 of period 407, each period checked to be exact. The same count holds near the branch point: its copies are Misiurewicz points whose orbits land on its fixed point α after 35 = 31 + 4 steps (two copies), 66 = 2·31 + 4 (four), 97 = 3·31 + 4 (eight) and 128 = 4·31 + 4 (sixteen), where the branch point itself lands after 4. Each doubling costs one more pass round the minibrot’s cycle.

Halfway to the minibrot

After k passes the orbit has reached about C raised to the power 2k−1. To leave at the same size after one more pass, |C| must be about the square root of what it was, so its logarithm halves. In plain terms: measured in powers of ten above the minibrot’s size, each ring is halfway between the previous ring and the minibrot. For the island, the nodes lie 103.06, 101.53, 100.77 and 100.39 times |s| from the center, each exponent about half the one before. Equivalently, each ring is at the geometric mean of the previous ring’s distance and the minibrot’s size: √(1.3·10−6 × 1.13·10−9) ≈ 3.8·10−8, the distance of the four nodes.

So the doublings come faster and faster: halfway to the minibrot, then a quarter of the way, an eighth, …, infinitely many before the minibrot is reached. Their symmetry fades as they crowd against the minibrot, whose outline the last rings take on, but the doubling of their number goes on forever. Deep zoomers use the rule to judge depths. Measured from the size of the period-3 minibrot, 0.019, the island is about 103.4 to 103.7 times smaller and its central minibrot 107.2 times: the minibrot lies at about twice the depth of its embedded Julia set. Claude Heiland-Allen put it this way: “what you have passed before appears again, only this time doubled up and twice the speed” (Two spirals out, 2015).

Artists exploit this. Zoom toward a shape, then turn off toward a minibrot nearby: the shape reappears doubled around the minibrot. Repeat, and the doublings stack into trees, lattices and other designs that no single location would show. Munafo calls this art Julia morphing, Heiland-Allen “building a tree”, and each new level lies about one and a half times as deep as the one before: the levels of Heiland-Allen’s 2015 zoom are about 10−128, 10−192 and 10−288 wide. His notes on patterns in embedded Julia sets also follow the nodes inside one island.

What is proved

The argument above approximates twice: the return map by a quadratic and one filled Julia set by another. Theorems make it exact up to a controlled distortion. Adrien Douady, Xavier Buff, Robert Devaney and Pierre Sentenac proved the first case in 2000: near the cusp of a primitive minibrot, small copies of M appear, each surrounded by nested copies of a slightly perturbed cauliflower Julia set and of its preimages under z ↦ z2m (Baby Mandelbrot sets are born in cauliflowers).

Tomoki Kawahira and Masashi Kisaka proved the general case (Julia sets appear quasiconformally in the Mandelbrot set, arXiv 2018, revised 2024). Take any minibrot and any Misiurewicz or parabolic parameter c*. Arbitrarily close to the point of the minibrot that corresponds to c*, the Mandelbrot set contains a quasiconformal copy of the Julia set of a parameter c* + η just outside M, a Cantor set; inside it the nested square-root copies, which they call its decoration; and at the center a smaller minibrot. Since such parameters are dense in the boundary, the same holds for every boundary point. Their Theorem B is the comparison of the figure above: for every parameter of the central minibrot, its filled Julia set around 0 contains the same nested structure. They also show that some copies are almost conformal: every disk that meets the boundary of M contains nearly undistorted copies of Cantor Julia sets. A sequel gives a second proof following Douady’s ideas (arXiv 2025), and Wolf Jung’s unfinished draft describes the levels and nodes in detail (Renormalization and embedded Julia sets, 2020). As of October 2026, the main paper is available as an arXiv preprint.

Key ideas

  • An embedded Julia set is an island of filaments that looks like a Julia set, with a minibrot at its center.
  • Near the center c0 of a minibrot of period p, the orbit of 0 returns close to 0 after p steps, zp(c) ≈ b′(c − c0), so the Mandelbrot set there is a shrunk and turned copy of the filled Julia set of c0 around the critical point 0.
  • Around 0, every quadratic Julia set is symmetric under a half turn; this is the island’s two-fold symmetry.
  • Each further pass of the orbit near 0 squares, and a square has two roots, so features repeat 2, 4, 8, 16, … times around the minibrot; the periods of the nodes grow by p from ring to ring.
  • In powers of ten, each ring lies halfway between the previous one and the minibrot, and the minibrot lies at about twice the depth of the island.
  • Kawahira and Kisaka proved that quasiconformal copies of Cantor Julia sets, with their nested square roots and a minibrot at the center, occur near every boundary point of every minibrot.

Check yourself

  1. The eight nodes of the third ring have period 306 and lie about 100.77 |s| from the center, with |s| ≈ 1.13·10−9. What period do the nodes of the next ring have, how many are there, and how far from the center do you expect them?

    One more pass adds 101 steps: period 407, and twice as many nodes, 16. The exponent halves: 100.385 |s| ≈ 2.7·10−9. The figure’s sixteen nodes lie 2.2–3.4·10−9 from the center; the spread comes from the minibrot’s outline, which the ring follows.

  2. A minibrot of size 10−12 lies 10−4 from a spiral. At what distances do the rings of two and of four spirals appear?

    At the geometric means: √(10−4 × 10−12) = 10−8 for two, then √(10−8 × 10−12) = 10−10 for four, and 10−11 for eight. The exponent is halfway to −12 each time.

  3. Why is the pattern around a minibrot symmetric under a half turn, while the pattern around a Misiurewicz point usually is not?

    Around a minibrot, the Mandelbrot set copies the filled Julia set of its center around the critical point 0, where z and −z have the same image. Around a Misiurewicz point it copies the Julia set around the critical value c, which has no such symmetry.

  4. In the comparison figure, why is the Julia picture 0.4969 wide, and why is it turned?

    The map c ↦ z101(c) ≈ b′(c − c0) carries the parameter plane near c0 to the dynamical plane near 0. It multiplies distances by |b′| ≈ 70,982, and 70,982 × 7·10−6 ≈ 0.4969, and it turns by the argument of b′, 112.4°.

  5. Is the doubling of the arms a period-doubling cascade?

    No. All the rings surround one minibrot of period 101, and the nodes’ periods grow by 101 per ring: 104, 205, 306, 407. A period-doubling cascade is a chain of components of periods p, 2p, 4p, …, each attached to the one before.
