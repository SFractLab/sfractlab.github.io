An illustrated atlas of complex dynamics

Two worlds in one equation

Square a complex number, add a constant, and repeat. That small instruction creates the Mandelbrot set, an entire family of Julia sets, and a landscape of cycles, islands, spirals, branching points, and unresolved mathematics.

The equation behind the Mandelbrot set and Julia sets

fc(z) = z2 + c,    zn+1 = fc(zn).

The essential distinction

A complex number is a point in a plane, with its real and imaginary parts as coordinates. So both kinds of set are drawn as pictures in a plane: the Mandelbrot set in the plane of all values of c, the parameter plane, and each Julia set in the plane of all starting points z0, the dynamical plane.

Parameter plane · vary c

The Mandelbrot set

Start with z0 = 0. For which values of the constant c do the numbers zn stay bounded? These values of c form one set in the parameter plane.

Dynamical plane · vary z0

A Julia set

Fix c. For which starting points z0 do the numbers zn stay bounded, and where is the boundary between bounded and escaping behavior?

The sequence z0, z1, z2, z3, … is called the orbit of z0. The orbit stays bounded if the distance of zn from 0 stays below some fixed number for every n, and it escapes if that distance grows towards infinity. For z2 + c, every orbit does one or the other.

The starting points with bounded orbits form the filled Julia set Kc. Its boundary is the Julia set Jc. The filled set and its boundary are different objects, although pictures of the filled set are often loosely called “Julia sets.” A fundamental theorem links the two planes: Jc is connected exactly when c belongs to the Mandelbrot set.

A route through the atlas

The chapters build on one another in this order. Each one names the chapters it builds on under its title and marks its level with one to three dots, and most chapters end with key ideas and questions to check yourself.

Begin with the equation (introductory)

  1. Chaos, nature, and fractals sets the scene: deterministic chaos, where it occurs in nature, and fractal geometry, the ideas that meet in the Mandelbrot and Julia sets.
  2. Mathematical foundations introduces complex arithmetic, iteration, the critical point, and escape.
  3. Orbits and stability follows actual numbers through fixed points, cycles, preperiodic behavior, and escape, and explains when cycles attract.
  4. Julia sets fixes the map and looks at every starting point at once.
  5. The Mandelbrot set turns the critical orbit into a map of all Julia sets, and explains why it is connected and where Julia sets change.
  6. A short history follows the subject from a question about Newton’s method in 1879 to the computer pictures of the late 1970s and 1980s and the open problems of today.

Read the landscape (intermediate, with an introductory tour)

  1. Cardioids, bulbs, and periods derives the main cardioid and period-two disk, counts the components of each period, and explains how bulbs are attached.
  2. External rays and angles puts coordinates on the outside of the Mandelbrot set and of connected Julia sets, and reads the angles of the rays that land at their landmarks.
  3. Special points explores Misiurewicz points, parabolic parameters, the Feigenbaum point, and the resemblance between the Mandelbrot set and Julia sets near Misiurewicz points.
  4. Minibrots and self-similarity explains why small Mandelbrot copies occur, what “copy” really means, and how tuning acts on periods and angles.
  5. Neutral cycles explains the cycles with multipliers of modulus 1: parabolic flowers and implosion, and the number theory that decides between Siegel disks and Cremer points.
  6. A guided tour visits twelve places in the Mandelbrot set, from the cusp at 1/4 to the tip at −2, with live pictures and what is known about each.

Go deeper (intermediate and advanced)

  1. Area, dimension and famous numbers separates what is exact, what is proved and what is only estimated about the size and roughness of the Mandelbrot set, and collects the numbers hidden in it, from π to Feigenbaum’s constants.
  2. Beyond z2 + c changes one ingredient of the quadratic family at a time, from higher degrees and the tricorn to Newton’s method and the Burning Ship, and explains why copies of the Mandelbrot set appear in almost every family of rational maps.
  3. Angles, addresses and trees turns external angles into an exact arithmetic: which rays land together, internal addresses, Hubbard trees, the spider algorithm, and a model of the whole set (advanced).
  4. Open questions states what is unknown about the Mandelbrot set as of October 2026, from local connectivity to the area of its boundary, what Yoccoz and his successors proved, and the recent progress (advanced).

Reading the pictures

Every fractal picture in this atlas was computed for it; most are drawn live in your browser. Captions or status lines give the window shown and the number of iterations. The diagrams explain ideas; their captions say whether they are sketches or computed plots.

A computer follows each orbit for only finitely many steps, and only at finitely many points. An orbit that has not escaped by the last step may still escape later, thin filaments can fall between pixels, and a Julia set of zero area can be almost invisible. Computing the pictures explains how the images are made and what they can prove; the gallery’s image-method notes describe the few precomputed plates.

Proof, picture, and conjecture are different. A picture suggests structure; a theorem establishes it. The Mandelbrot set is connected, as Douady and Hubbard proved in 1982. Whether it is also locally connected, the MLC conjecture, is still open as of October 2026. This atlas marks open questions explicitly.

Scope and further study

The main subject is the complex quadratic family fc(z) = z² + c. Julia sets exist for much more general maps, but many properties described here, such as the dichotomy between connected sets and dust, fail for general maps; Beyond z2 + c explains which ones survive.
